Study routine

Repeatable process
  1. Pause the chart.

    Choose a historical point and stop before the next candles appear.

  2. Describe, don't predict.

    Write observable facts: swing highs/lows, candle ranges, gaps, or changes in volatility.

  3. Write a question.

    State what evidence could challenge your first interpretation.

  4. Reveal the next candles.

    Record what actually appeared, including outcomes that surprised you.

  5. Keep the takeaway specific.

    Separate what the chart showed from what you assumed it meant.

Lesson 1 · Market structure and BOS

A break of structure is a move through a previously identified swing level. Define whether your rule uses a wick or a candle close before you test it.

  1. Mark a clear swing high and low using one consistent pivot rule.
  2. Write down what counts as a break: wick through, candle close, or another objective condition.
  3. Record what would invalidate the idea and what would make you skip it.
Replay task: find 20 historical examples and record every valid and invalid break, not only the clean ones.

Lesson 2 · Fair value gaps (FVG) and inverse FVG (IFVG)

An FVG is commonly defined using a three-candle price imbalance. Definitions differ, so use the exact rule you plan to test.

  1. Define the three-candle boundaries precisely and mark the zone without looking ahead.
  2. Record whether price returns to the zone and what your predefined reaction condition is.
  3. For an IFVG, write down exactly what invalidates the original zone and how you identify the inversion.
Replay task: log at least 20 examples and note context, session, outcome, and costs. A gap is not a guaranteed reversal or entry signal.

Lesson 3 · Drawing and testing trendlines

Trendlines summarize swing points; they do not predict price. Avoid adjusting them after seeing the outcome.

  1. Pick two meaningful rising swing lows to draw a candidate line.
  2. Decide in advance if a wick or candle close defines a break; don't move the line to rescue the setup.
  3. If testing a retest, define what confirmation means and what invalidates it.
Replay task: mark 20 charts before revealing the next candles. Record false breaks as well as successful examples.

Lesson 4 · Risk, costs, and a repeatable review

  1. Know the instrument's contract multiplier and tick value from official specifications; don't guess.
  2. Record planned stop, target logic, fees, and realistic slippage assumptions.
  3. After each session, review rule-following and execution separately from the result.
  4. Change one testable variable at a time and compare enough examples before drawing conclusions.
Learning sequence: understand the concept → define objective rules → replay historical examples → paper trade consistently → review the journal. These lessons are educational and not trade signals.
COURSE 05 · FOUNDATIONS

Support, resistance & price zones

Learn to mark areas where price previously paused or changed direction. A zone is an observation—not a promise that price will react the same way again.

Visual lesson

How to study it

  1. Zoom out and identify obvious historical turning points instead of marking every tiny candle.
  2. Draw a zone around the area where several reactions clustered; avoid pretending the level is exact to one price.
  3. Label the zone using only information visible at that moment, then move forward candle by candle in replay.
  4. Record whether price paused, crossed through, or returned later. Include examples where the zone did not matter.
Common mistake: drawing a perfect zone after seeing what happened next. Mark first, reveal later, and keep the original drawing for an honest review.
COURSE 06 · MARKET CONTEXT

Trend, range & uncertainty

Before labeling a pattern, describe the environment. Similar shapes can behave differently in a directional move, a sideways range, or a messy transition.

Visual lesson

Context checklist

  • Trend: are successive swing highs and lows generally moving in one direction?
  • Range: does price repeatedly move between recognizable upper and lower areas?
  • Transition: has the prior pattern weakened, but a new structure not yet become clear?
  • Uncertainty: would two careful reviewers agree on the label? If not, record “unclear” rather than forcing a setup.
Practice: label 30 historical chart sections as trend, range, or unclear before revealing future candles. Compare your labels with a second review to find where your definitions are vague.
COURSE 07 · PATTERN QUALITY

Breakouts, retests & false breaks

A breakout describes price moving beyond a previously marked boundary. It can continue, return inside the area, or reverse. The purpose of this lesson is to classify outcomes consistently—not to predict the next move.

Visual lesson

Build an objective definition

  1. Mark the boundary before reviewing what happens next.
  2. Choose one definition for “break” (for example, a candle close beyond the boundary) and keep it unchanged for the whole study.
  3. Define a fixed observation window, such as the next 5 candles, so examples are comparable.
  4. Classify each case: held outside, returned inside, retested boundary, or unclear. Do not delete inconvenient examples.
  5. Note timeframe, instrument, date, market context, fees assumptions if relevant, and screenshot reference.
A clean-looking example is not evidence of an advantage. Compare all cases in your journal and treat a small sample as preliminary.
COURSE 08 · RESEARCH WORKFLOW

Turn an idea into a fair historical study

Use this workflow to learn whether your written definition is clear and repeatable. Results are descriptive; they cannot guarantee future performance.

01 · DEFINE

Write the rule first

Specify context, pattern boundaries, confirmation definition, invalidation, skip conditions, and what counts as one example.

02 · COLLECT

Keep every case

Review consecutive historical examples, including messy ones and failures. Avoid selecting only memorable charts.

03 · RECORD

Use the same fields

Capture date, instrument, timeframe, setup label, screenshot, rule-following, outcome, and known costs.

04 · REVIEW

Describe, don't predict

Look for missing data, inconsistent labels, and differences between conditions. Test one change at a time on a fresh sample.

Keep a “did not qualify” section too. It helps you see whether your rules are clear and whether you are changing them after seeing the outcome.

Trade calculation settings

Universal P&L model: (exit − entry) × quantity × value per 1.00 move × direction − fees. For stocks and spot assets, the value-per-move is usually based on units; for options, futures, CFDs, and other derivatives, confirm the contract multiplier with your platform. This journal does not assume every asset uses the same math.
